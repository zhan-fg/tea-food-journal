## What changed this time

The first two attempts used whatever red dates were in the cupboard. This time I specifically bought **Loulan-brand (楼兰) gray dates** — more expensive, but reputedly much better quality.

## Experiment parameters

- **Date variety**: Loulan gray dates (若羌灰枣)
- **Quantity**: 10 dates
- **Water**: 1000ml
- **Boil time**: 10 minutes
- **Steep time**: 4 hours
- **Steep temperature**: 60°C (kettle keep-warm setting)

## Results

### Compared to previous attempts

| Dimension | Attempt #1 | Attempt #2 | Attempt #3 (this one) |
|-----------|------------|------------|------------------------|
| Date type | Hebei jujubes | Generic gray dates | Loulan gray dates |
| Steep time | 2 hours | 3 hours | 4 hours |
| Sweetness | ★★☆ | ★★★☆ | ★★★★☆ |
| Clarity | Cloudy | Okay | Clear |
| Overall | Fine | Good | Great |

## Takeaways

1. **Date quality is everything.** Good variety + good brand = an entirely different tea. Loulan dates are about 50% pricier than supermarket dates, but worth it.

2. **Three hours minimum for steeping.** The jump from 2 hours to 4 is dramatic. Four hours hits a sweetness plateau that two hours never reaches.

3. **Low-temp steeping beats high-temp boiling.** Continuous boiling actually drives off some volatile sweetness. The keep-warm setting at ~60°C does the job gently.

4. **Breaking them open is non-negotiable.** I left one whole date in the pot as a control — when I bit into it afterward, the inside was still bland.

## Next experiment ideas
