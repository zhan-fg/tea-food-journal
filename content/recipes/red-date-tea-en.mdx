## Steps

### Step 1: Prep the dates

![Prepping red dates for tea](/images/recipes/red-date-tea-01.jpg)

Rinse 10 red dates. **Critical: break each date open** so the flesh is exposed. This is what allows the sweetness to infuse into the water.

### Step 2: Bring to a boil

![Bringing red dates and water to a boil](/images/recipes/red-date-tea-02.jpg)

Place the broken dates in your electric kettle with 1000ml of water. Bring to a boil.

### Step 3: Boil for 10 minutes

![Boiling red dates for 10 minutes](/images/recipes/red-date-tea-03.jpg)

Let it boil for 10 minutes to start the extraction. You'll see the water turn a pale amber.

### Step 4: The magic step — low-temp steep

![Steeping red date tea on keep-warm mode](/images/recipes/red-date-tea-04.jpg)

This is where it all happens. Switch your kettle to "keep warm" mode (around 60–80°C / 140–175°F) and leave it for 4–5 hours. A long, gentle steep draws out far more sweetness and depth than continuous boiling ever could.

### Step 5: Enjoy

![A cup of finished red date tea](/images/recipes/red-date-tea-05.jpg)

The result is a clear amber tea that tastes naturally sweet — no sugar, no honey, just red dates doing their thing. You can refill with water 2–3 times; the second steep is still remarkably sweet.
